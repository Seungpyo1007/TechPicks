Decide, don't browse.

01 — What the app does today

Five tabs are five catalogs. Nothing in the app remembers what you are trying to decide.

Home is eight circular icons, a search field labelled “Where to?” and a “Set destination on map” row — leftovers from a maps template.

Rankings open a webview of nanoreview.net, handing the user to a website at the moment of highest intent.

Comparison is a radial chart of one phone at a time, and the AI chat is behind an unmarked swipe-up.

02 — What I changed

The unit of the app is a decision, not a category. A shortlist persists across every screen and every screen can add to it.

One number, stated openly: the TP Index — performance 25, camera 25, display 20, battery 20, value 10. Never shown without its parts.

Compare is a tab, not a chart: two devices, one table, the winning cell in red. Rankings come back in-app as a sortable table.

Scan and the 3D viewer — your two roadmap items — sit where intent already is: Scan in the header of every screen, 3D on the product page.

03 — Assumptions to check

Data is the 2026 flagship set and assumes your planned TechPicks API, not nanoreview webviews.

Prices are USD MSRP; KRW appears when the locale is Korean.

Product photography is a drop slot — drag real images onto the grey blocks and they stick.

Anonymous sign-in stays: it is the shortest path to a shortlist. Google / Apple / email remain.
